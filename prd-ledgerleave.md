# PRD: Ledgerleave — PTO & Approvals

**Sample business:** "Ledgerleave," HR-lite for a synthetic 50-person company (15 hourly, 35 salaried; CEO → 4 VPs → managers → individual contributors; two HR role holders), three leave policies, two years of leave (2024–2025)
**Build log:** #14 PTO & Approvals, main track. Chips: accrual rules · carryover caps · approval chains · delegation
**Status:** Draft v1.0 — kickoff kit
**Learning objectives:** two deterministic engines that nothing else in the portfolio needs — (1) an **accrual engine** that derives a leave balance at any date by replaying an append-only ledger under policy rules held as data (accrual frequency, tenure tiers, hire-date proration, carryover cap at a policy-year boundary, max-balance cap, negative-balance flag, rounding rule), and (2) an **approval-routing engine** that turns a request into an ordered chain of required approvals from a routing table, with out-of-office delegation, self-approval and HR fallbacks, a chain frozen at submit, and ordered reason codes on every decision; both measured against a Python oracle entry for entry and chain for chain

**Positioning note:** the HR-lite surface (employees, requests, an inbox) is the floor. The lesson is that a balance is a *derivation* and an approval chain is a *routing decision*, both explainable and both reproducible from data. `docs/portfolio-overlap.md` lists what is ported (append-only ledger discipline from LoanBook, calendar-axis dates from Bookable, rules-as-data and reason traces from Adjuster, the outbox from Callboard) and what is new.

`docs/decisions.md` outranks this document wherever they differ.

## ⚠️ Scope Honesty (read first)

Learning project, synthetic data only. Every employee, hire date, timesheet, delegation and request is generated by `tools/build_fixtures.py` from a seed; names come from fixed lists and no real person or company is represented. The three leave policies are *sample rules*: the sick-leave policy has the shape of a statutory accrual floor (one fixed amount per pay period, a carryover cap, a max balance) but is not any jurisdiction's law, and this project gives no HR, payroll, tax or employment-law advice. No payroll integration, no calendar sync, no email: approval requests queue to an outbox and are never sent. The numbers this project reports — ledger entries, balances, chains, diffs — are agreement between a TypeScript implementation and a Python reference on fixtures written by the same author, never "correct" without the number. Banner verbatim in the README.

## Problem Statement

A 50-person company tracks leave in a spreadsheet. Balances are typed in by hand after each payroll, so nobody can say what a balance *was* on a given date or why it is what it is now; a cancelled vacation is "fixed" by editing the number, and the year-end carryover is a column someone overwrites in January. Approvals go by email: a manager who is out means a request sits, a manager who is also the requester approves their own leave, and nobody can later say who approved what or whether they were allowed to.

Ledgerleave treats a balance as the sum of an append-only ledger (accruals the policy generated, usage a request approval wrote, reversals a cancellation wrote, forfeitures a year boundary wrote) and an approval as a routing decision from a table, so both are reproducible and both carry their reasons. The builder lesson: time-based entitlements are a replay problem, not a stored number; and routing is a pure function of the org state at submit time, frozen so later changes cannot rewrite history.

## Goals

1. **A balance at any as-of date is derived**, never stored: `balance(employee, type, asOf)` sums entries effective on or before the date; `ledger:verify` recomputes every employee-year from entries and matches the oracle to the minute.
2. **Every accrual, forfeiture and usage is a ledger row with a reason**, and policy rules are data: three policies with different frequencies, year types, caps and rounding all run through one engine.
3. **Every routing decision is an ordered chain with ordered reason codes**, deterministic in the org state at submit time, and frozen afterwards.
4. **Deliberate traps are built in and proven**: mid-period hire, tier boundary mid-year, leap day, request spanning the year boundary, cancellation after year-end, max-balance cap reopened by usage, hourly → salaried transfer, chained delegation, delegate who is the requester, manager terminated, manager changed mid-flight, HR role revoked mid-flight.
5. **(Builder goal)** Know exactly where TypeScript and Python disagree about calendars, rounding and ordering — and make the count 0 on the frozen fixtures.

## Non-Goals

- **Payroll, payout at termination, leave encashment, FMLA/statutory compliance tracking.** The ledger freezes at termination (O-7).
- **Calendar or email integration.** Approval notifications are queued to the outbox and never sent.
- **Half-day or hourly requests.** Requests are whole working days of 480 minutes (O-5).
- **Overlap detection, blackout dates, team-coverage rules, holiday calendars per location.** One company holiday list; overlap validation is Phase 2 (O-6); fixtures contain no overlaps.
- **Scheduled jobs.** Accruals are posted by replay from policy data, not by a cron that mutates balances; a nightly "post today's accruals" job is a Phase 2 seam that writes the same rows the replay would.
- **LLM anywhere in the engines** (O-8). A policy-JSON explainer is a P1 experiment.
- **Multi-tenant billing, public API, SSO.** Foundation orgs as cloned.

## Personas

- **Priya, HR manager** — owns the policies. Edits policy JSON in a validated editor, reads an employee's ledger line by line when someone disputes a balance, reassigns a stuck approval step, and needs the year-end forfeiture to be a row she can point at, not a number that changed.
- **Marcus, engineering manager** — approves his team's requests from an inbox, is out for two weeks a year with a delegation set, and must not be able to approve his own leave.
- **Dana, support agent (hourly)** — accrues PTO per hour worked, moved to salaried mid-2025, and wants to see before submitting who will approve and whether the balance covers it.

## Requirements — Must-Have (P0)

**P0-1: Org, policies, assignments (the floor)**
- [ ] `Employee` (id, name, title, dept, `managerId`, `employmentType`, roles, `hireDate`, `termDate`), org changes as dated rows (`MANAGER`, `ROLE_GRANTED`, `ROLE_REVOKED`); org state at a date is derived from the base row plus changes effective on or before it (brief §2)
- [ ] `Policy` as JSON data validated against the closed vocabularies in brief §3 (frequency, proration, rounding, year type); `PolicyAssignment` per employee and leave type with start/end dates; one employee may move between policies of the same leave type (transfer, D-16)
- [ ] Holidays and pay periods (anchor + length) as data; working days = Mon–Fri minus holidays (C1)
- [ ] Every table clone-owned under its own Prisma schema file; immutability triggers as hand-written SQL (Phase 2)

**P0-2: Accrual engine** *(core learning artifact #1)* — `src/accrual/`, pure
- [ ] Accrual events A1–A5: frequency `MONTHLY` (last day of month, `DAILY` proration by active days in the month, `HALF_UP`), `PER_PAY_PERIOD` (full amount when active on the period end), `PER_HOURS_WORKED` (minutes worked × rate ÷ 60, `FLOOR`), `ANNIVERSARY` (on each clamped anniversary); tier chosen by completed months of tenure at the event date (A2)
- [ ] Max-balance cap: an accrual posts `min(gross, cap − balance as of its date)` and records the shortfall as `cappedMinutes` (A5); a zero accrual is still a row
- [ ] Year boundaries A6 per policy year type (`CALENDAR` Dec 31, `POLICY_YEAR` the day before `starts`, `ANNIVERSARY` the clamped anniversary); forfeiture = `max(0, balance at end of boundary day − carryover cap)`, posted after that day's accruals and request events
- [ ] Retroactive entries (a reversal or a late approval effective in a closed policy year) recompute forfeiture from that boundary on and post the delta as one `CARRYOVER_RECOMPUTED` row (A7); accruals are never recomputed
- [ ] Transfer between policies of one leave type: `TRANSFER_OUT`/`TRANSFER_IN` pair, zero-sum, the new policy governs from its start date (D-16)
- [ ] GA-01..GA-20 reproduced exactly: entries, minutes, codes, balances

**P0-3: Ledger and balance** *(core learning artifact #2)*
- [ ] `LedgerEntry` kinds exactly `ACCRUAL`, `USAGE`, `USAGE_REVERSAL`, `ADJUSTMENT`, `FORFEITURE`, `TRANSFER_OUT`, `TRANSFER_IN`; fields per brief §5; `effective` and `posted` dates both kept
- [ ] `balance(asOf)` = Σ minutes effective ≤ asOf (L1); `available` = Σ all posted minutes (L2); neither is stored
- [ ] Usage is one `USAGE` row per working day of an approved request, effective that day (D-8); cancellation writes one `USAGE_REVERSAL` per usage row, effective the same day, posted on the cancel date (S4)
- [ ] `ledger:verify` recomputes per employee-year `opening, accrued, capped, used, forfeited, balance_end` and diffs against `fixtures/expected_balances.csv`

**P0-4: Approval routing engine** *(core learning artifact #3)* — `src/routing/`, pure
- [ ] Routing table as data: steps `MANAGER` (always), `SKIP_LEVEL` (working days > policy `skip_level_over_days`), `HR` (working days > policy `hr_over_days`); R1–R9 in brief §6: CEO's manager is the HR primary, inactive approver climbs to their manager, self-approval goes to the requester's manager, one-hop delegation, depth exceeded → fallback (HR primary → other HR → CEO), delegate who is the requester drops the step, duplicate approvers collapse, empty chain → `FALLBACK` step
- [ ] Every step carries ordered reason codes from the closed set in brief §6; dropped steps are kept in the output marked dropped
- [ ] The chain is frozen at submit (R9); `leave:route` prints it before submit
- [ ] GR-01..GR-15 reproduced exactly as chain strings

**P0-5: Request state machine** *(core learning artifact #4)*
- [ ] States `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → PENDING(k)* → APPROVED | DENIED | CANCELLED` with the transition table in brief §7; `SUBMIT` freezes the chain and denies at once with `INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE` when the policy forbids negative balance and `available − requested < 0` (A8)
- [ ] Eligibility at approval time (S3): actor is the step's current approver, is employed on that date, and holds the HR role if the step selected an HR holder; otherwise the event is **void** and logged with its code; `REASSIGN` by an HR holder changes a pending step's approver
- [ ] Events on terminal requests are logged as ignored and change nothing (S6); `CANCEL` from `APPROVED` is allowed to the requester or HR at any time and writes reversals
- [ ] The full request log (`ACTION:actor:date:code`) is reproduced for all 389 fixture requests

**P0-6: Evaluation** *(the measured claim)*
- [ ] `npm run leave:eval` replays `fixtures/` through both engines and prints the brief §11 summary (entries by kind, accrual codes, recomputes, requests by final state and code, kept steps per request, routing codes, void and ignored events, per-year totals by leave type) and the per employee-year table; with `--requests`, per request (working days, minutes, frozen chain, final state, log); exits non-zero on any diff against `fixtures/ledger.csv`, `expected_balances.csv`, `expected_requests.csv`
- [ ] Target stated as a target: **0 diffs** on all three files at the frozen hashes in brief §12. Measured values go in `docs/EVAL.md` with the fixture hash

**P0-7: Writes, triggers, outbox (Phase 2)**
- [ ] Ledger and request-event tables append-only by trigger; a request's chain stored as submitted; approvals, denials, cancellations, reassignments as event rows that the engine replays
- [ ] Outbox: `APPROVAL_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED` notifications queued per step, never sent
- [ ] Idempotent posting: replaying the fixture events into the database twice writes nothing the second time; `ledger:verify` against the database equals the oracle

**P0-8: Views (Phase 3)**
- [ ] **Employee balance page:** balance and available per leave type, the ledger line by line (kind, effective, posted, minutes, capped, codes, ref), filter by year
- [ ] **Request form:** dates → working days and minutes, predicted chain with reason codes shown before submit, balance check result
- [ ] **Approver inbox:** pending steps for me (including delegated), approve/deny with the eligibility verdict shown; HR reassign
- [ ] **HR policy editor:** policy JSON validated against the vocabularies, diff against the active version, assignments per employee; year-boundary preview per policy
- [ ] **Outbox view** of queued notifications

## Risks

- **Calendar disagreement.** Python's `date` and a hand-written TypeScript calendar can differ on week-day, month length and clamped anniversaries. Mitigation: C-rules with goldens (GA-03, GA-12, GA-19); CI under a non-UTC `TZ`.
- **Ordering within a day.** The replay order (transfers → accruals → request events → boundaries) decides several goldens. Mitigation: D-9 states it; GA-06/GA-14 fail if it moves.
- **Retroactive recompute scope.** Recomputing forfeiture but not accruals is a policy choice that can look inconsistent. Mitigation: D-10 states it and why; GA-10 proves it; O-4 keeps the alternative open.
- **Routing edge explosion.** Every new rule multiplies cases. Mitigation: nine rules, closed code set, fifteen goldens; a new code needs a decision and a golden.
- **Scope creep into HR software.** Mitigation: Phase 3 is the last P0 phase and its task list is written only at the Phase 2 gate.
